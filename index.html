Product & HS Code Architecture

ITC HS: 1302.32.20 / 130232

Agricultural Product Identity

Guar Gum is derived from the endosperm of the drought-hardy cluster bean seed (Cyamopsis tetragonoloba). Over 70% of global cultivation takes place in the arid fields of Western Rajasthan (Jodhpur, Bikaner, Nagaur, Barmer), with Haryana and Gujarat contributing the rest.

Harvest Season: October – December (Kharif crop).

Customs Classification

  • 130232: Mucilages and thickeners, whether or not modified, derived from locust beans, locust bean seeds or guar seeds.
  • 1302.32.20: Guar gum treated and pulverized (Food/Technical grade).
  • 1302.32.30: Chemically modified guar gum.

Target Export Grade

Refined Food-Grade E412 Guar Gum: 200 mesh particle size, rapid cold-water hydration, 5,000–6,000 cps viscosity (1% aqueous solution). Acts as a clean-label texturizer, moisture retention stabilizer in vegan foods, dairy, bakery, and sauces.

Target Segment: Food Ingredients (E412)
Strategic Focus: While India traditionally exports massive bulk volumes of low-margin fast-hydrating industrial guar for oilfield hydraulic fracturing (fracking), this export business model targets premium food-grade E412 powder. This provides higher, defensible margins ($3,200/MT vs $1,600/MT industrial) and complete insulation from volatile crude oil prices.

Why is India's unit value ($1,950) below world average ($2,650)?
HS 130232 merges Guar gum with Mediterranean Locust Bean Gum (Carob) from Spain/Italy. Carob trades at an astronomical $18,000+/MT due to tree supply shortages, pulling up the world average. Our food-grade guar ($3,200/MT) is positioned as an economical, clean-label substitute.

Top 3 Rival Exporters Analysis

1. Pakistan (Direct Competitor) 7.9% Share | $65.2M

Competes in raw splits and standard industrial grade. Has lower technological capabilities for fine mesh food pulverization and lack of European audit certifications.

2. Spain (Substitute Competitor) 13.5% Share | $110.4M

Supplies Locust Bean Gum (LBG) at $18,000+/MT. Chronic droughts in Mediterranean groves create severe supply deficits, driving food formulators to switch to Indian food-grade guar.

3. China (Downstream Re-processor) 6.3% Share | $52.0M

Imports raw guar splits from India, performs chemical derivatization into hydroxypropyl and cationic guar, and re-exports at high margins.

14 Non-Tariff Measures (NTMs): The 3 That Matter Most

NTM 1: SPS Emergency Control

Commission Reg (EU) 2019/1793

Mandatory testing for Pentachlorophenol (PCP) and Dioxins. Every Indian shipment must carry an official Health Certificate from the Export Inspection Council (EIC) confirming PCP < 0.01 mg/kg.

Action: Dual pre-shipment GC-MS testing via Eurofins/EIC approved labs.
NTM 2: SPS Chemical Residue

Ethylene Oxide (EtO) Prohibition

EU Regulation (EC) 396/2005 sets a strict limit of < 0.02 mg/kg for EtO. Multiple Indian food consignments have faced rapid border rejections due to EtO fumigation.

Action: 100% ban on gas fumigation; utilize validated dry steam sterilization.
NTM 3: TBT Food Additive

Commission Reg (EU) No 231/2012

Strict purity criteria for food additive E412: Galactomannan > 75%, Acid-insoluble matter < 7%, Total protein < 10%, Arsenic < 3ppm, Lead < 2ppm.

Action: In-house automated NIR spectroscopy batch grading in Jodhpur mill.

Route & Logistics Milestones (32–36 Days)

  1. Processing & Lab Testing (Days 1–4): Jodhpur Mill, Rajasthan. Automated pulverization, 200 mesh grading, pre-shipment EIC test.
  2. Inland Rail Pre-Carriage (Days 5–7): Bhagat Ki Kothi ICD (Jodhpur) to Port of Mundra, Gujarat via dedicated freight corridor.
  3. Ocean Transit (Days 8–34): Port of Mundra to Port of Hamburg, Germany (via container carrier, e.g. Hapag-Lloyd/Maersk).
  4. Customs Clearance & Storage (Days 35–37): Hamburg Port Border Inspection Post (BIP) clearance; transfer to temperature-controlled bonded warehouse in Bremen.

Critical Risks & Contingency Actions

1. Pesticide/EtO Border Alert: Dual-sample archive system; hold pre-cleared backup lots in Hamburg warehouse.
2. Raw Seed Crop Drought in Rajasthan: Procure 3-month raw split inventory during peak post-harvest arrivals (Nov–Dec) + NCDEX commodity hedging.
3. Red Sea Maritime Freight Rerouting: Hold 60-day safety stock buffer at German distribution hub to ensure uninterrupted client supply.
4. Foreign Buyer Insolvency: Comprehensive ECGC export credit insurance covering 90% of commercial invoice value.
